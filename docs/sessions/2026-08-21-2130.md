## Why "a tie" is actually a loss

To profit as a taker you must beat the market by more than the fee. The
Kalshi taker fee is 1.75¢ per contract at 50¢, ~0.33¢ at 95¢.

Our measured advantage is **Δ ≈ 0, trending slightly negative**. A model
that merely matches the price pays the fee on every entry for nothing.
That is not a marginal result to be improved by tuning — it is the
structural condition under which taker directional trading cannot work.

Paper P&L across the sweep runs −$20.70 to +$2.52 on ~$100. At 66–96
trades that is noise, and it is the wrong metric anyway, but nothing in it
contradicts the Brier finding.

## Recommendation

The revival plan set the exit criterion itself: *"≥300 settled
observations. If no slice beats the market, the directional book is dead
and only §2(c) maker capture survives."*

At 196/300 the answer is not yet formally in, but the trajectory has been
consistent and the strongest sub-thesis (final-minute TWAP) is flat with a
tight interval over 194 windows. I would let the collector run to 300 —
it costs nothing but wall-clock, and a falsification is only worth having
if it is complete — while treating the directional book as very likely
dead.

**Do not spend more effort tuning the directional model.** Three tuned
candidates have already evaporated, and the one real defect (sigma too
low) does not convert into edge when corrected.

What survives, in the plan's own order:

- **§2(c) maker-side capture.** 0% maker fee against a 1.75¢ taker fee is
  a real structural subsidy, and binary inventory self-liquidates every 15
  minutes. This harness cannot evaluate it — replay models taker fills
  only, with no queue position — so it needs its own instrument.
- The recording/replay/scoring stack, which is venue-agnostic and is the
  actual asset built here.
