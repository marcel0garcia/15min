## The one finding

**The model is over-confident in both tails.** From `score --all --calibration`:

| Predicted | N | Mean p | Realized |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.0–0.1 | 19 | 0.036 | **0.263** |
| 0.1–0.2 | 10 | 0.150 | 0.500 |
| 0.2–0.3 | 18 | 0.255 | 0.611 |
| 0.6–0.7 | 37 | 0.645 | 0.432 |
| 0.8–0.9 | 56 | 0.848 | 0.696 |
| 0.9–1.0 | **155** | 0.975 | **0.865** |

The distribution is too narrow. That is the mechanical signature of a
sigma that is too low, and it is consistent with `sigma_floor: 0.20`
against BTC realized vol of 0.40–0.60.

The sweep agrees, independently — it re-prices every scan rather than
reading the recorded probability:

| `sigma_scale` | Trades/day | Δ vs market | Paper P&L |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.0 | 79.3 | +0.0022 | −$6.11 |
| 1.5 | 58.4 | +0.0135 | +$1.19 |
| 2.0 | 41.7 | **+0.0169** | **+$3.84** |
| 2.5 | 29.2 | +0.0171 | +$0.05 |

Monotone in edge, and the knee is near 2.0: past that, frequency halves
for essentially no further edge. Note this is exactly the edge/frequency
trade-off — the operating point is a choice, and 2.0 keeps ~42
entries/day.

**No config change made.** Every CI straddles zero at n=17. Two
independent lines of evidence pointing the same way is a reason to keep
collecting, not a reason to act.

## Bugs found and fixed

Each was found by running the thing, not by reading it.

1. **Entry 0.2s after startup.** Sigma pinned to its 0.20 floor with no
   BRTI history; model priced 0.996 against a market at 0.905; bought 10
   contracts at 91¢; flipped out four seconds later at a loss once real
   ticks arrived. Both legs were sigma noise. → `warmup_sec`,
   `reject_clamped_sigma`.
2. **The sigma floor was applied twice** — per-leg and again after
   blending — so `clamped` always came back `False` and the guard in (1)
   could never have fired. → clamp once, at the end.
3. **`score`'s bootstrap resampled scans, not markets.** ~36 views of one
   coin flip counted as 36 coin flips; intervals came out ~6× too narrow.
   A slice could have reached `enabled_slices`, and real money, on five
   markets. → cluster bootstrap over tickers; verdicts gate on
   `n_markets >= 30`.
4. **Phantom orderbook levels.** Deltas applied as floats leave residue
   (~8.8e-13) when a level is fully cancelled, so dead levels survived as
   the best bid — bid 41 above ask 36. The policy correctly refuses a
   crossed book, so this silently discarded **79 of 247 scans**. →
   epsilon threshold.
5. **Signal-only sessions logged `reject_gate: null` on every row**
   (528/528), because the policy only ran when trading was on. → the
   policy is pure, so it now shadow-evaluates every scan.
6. **`trades_per_day` exceeded the 96/day ceiling** — it divided by
   summed wall-clock span, which double-counts across merged sessions. →
   entries-per-market × 96.

Plus: `sigma_fast_sec` / `sigma_slow_sec` / `sigma_fast_weight` were in
`config.yaml` from day one and never reached the engine, which used
hard-coded defaults.

## Things established, with numbers

- **Our BRTI reconstruction is the right instrument.** Against 16 markets
  with official Kalshi results it agreed on 15 (94%). The one miss
  settled **$1.94** from strike — ~0.003% error on a $68.7k index. Every
  market settling more than $21 from strike agreed.
  → `resolve_outcomes` now refuses to call a settlement within $25 of the
  strike, because a coin-flip label is worse than no label.
- **Official outcomes decay.** Every June 2026 ticker now 404s. That
  ~1.5 GB of recordings is permanently unscoreable — and the only session
  with cached outcomes predates the `strike` field, so it cannot be
  re-priced either. The corpus started empty for this reason. Enrich
  promptly; the supervisor now does it between segments.
- **A locked book is normal.** `yes_bid == yes_ask` means the best YES
  and NO bids sum to exactly 100 — zero spread, fully collateralized, and
  tradeable. Treating it as corrupt rejected 60–95% of scans.

## Open, not understood

- **Sigma depends on our polling rate.** On one session (five evaluation
  points) sigma off the 4 Hz BRTI stream read ~0.84× the same data at
  1 Hz. A synthetic with three zero returns in four reproduces 0.99 — the
  estimator annualizes by observed inter-arrival time and self-corrects —
  so the obvious explanation is wrong and the mechanism is unknown.
  `core.sigma_sample_sec` ships as a no-op knob so `sweep` can settle it
  once a few hours of `brti_ticks` exist.
- **What causes the crossed books that remain.** The epsilon fix removes
  the float-residue case; anything left is a real delta-application gap.
- **`core.fee_rate` is still unverified** (0.07 is the *standard*-category
  rate; crypto may price above it). One small live fill settles it. Until
  then every EV number is provisional in our favour, which is the
  dangerous direction.
